**What this is.** A slice of the V2 track, built ahead of time in parallel with CLI V1, as a

## 1. FRAME

**Problem.** V2-3.3 picks and applies a generalization node, and its `SearchResult` states the
node and three loss figures. Nothing states the re-identification risk of the released table, and
nothing produces a document a person or a later tool can keep: the sprint's done-state is "a
frozen report (prosecutor, journalist and marketer risk as ARX defines them; information loss;
suppressed rows) attached to evidence, shown by the CLI". Slice 1 is the report itself.

**Guarantee (the whole promise of this slice).** For a table `T`, a `Lattice` `L`, a found
`SearchResult` `R` from `search_lattice(T, L, ...)`, the `Released` `D = apply_search(T, L, R)`, a
risk threshold `tau` in `(0, 1]` and optionally a population table `P`:

- G1 (prosecutor risk, El Emam; ARX `getProsecutorRisk`). Over the equivalence classes of
  `D.table` (the released rows, suppressed rows removed) with sizes `f_j`, `n = sum f_j`,
  `J` classes: highest risk `1 / min f_j`; average risk `J / n`; records at risk
  `sum {f_j : 1 / f_j > tau} / n`, with `tau` read as the decimal it prints as
  (`Fraction(repr(tau))`, as V2-3.3 reads the suppression limit).
- G2 (journalist and marketer risk, only with a population). When `P` is given, it is
  generalized to `R.node` with `L.apply`, and each released class `j` gets its population size
  `F_j` (rows of `P` in the same class, same class identity as V2-3.2). Journalist: highest
  `1 / min F_j`; average `max(J / sum F_j, (1 / n) sum f_j / F_j)`; records at risk
  `sum {f_j : 1 / F_j > tau} / n`. Marketer: `(1 / n) sum f_j / F_j`. These are ARX's
  `RiskModelSampleSummary` formulas with a supplied superset.
- G3 (no sample-only journalist or marketer figure). Without `P` the journalist and marketer
  sections are `null` and the document states `population: "not_supplied"`. No estimate from the
  sample alone is ever written under those names.
- G4 (utility and decision). The document carries, from `R` unchanged: the node, the lattice
  heights and size, nodes evaluated, the objective metric and all three loss figures
  (discernibility, loss metric, non-uniform entropy), the input row count, the suppression limit,
  the allowed and actual suppressed rows; plus rows released, class count and smallest class.
- G5 (models met, recomputed). For each model in `R.models`, the document states the model
  (kind token and thresholds) and that no class of the released rows fails it, recomputed through
  `FrequencySet` on `D.generalized_including_suppressed` over all input rows (V2-3.3's rule:
  t against the input distribution). If the recomputed suppressed rows differ from
  `D.suppressed`, or any released class fails a model, the build refuses (value-free
  `ValueError`): the inputs do not belong together.
- G6 (versioned, read back). `to_payload()` gives a JSON-compatible dict tagged
  `schema: "risk-utility-report/v1"`. The reader is derived from the writer (recurring defect 4):
  it refuses another major, a missing tag, free text where a token belongs and wrong-typed
  numbers; it accepts an added optional field and a token added in a later minor. A synthetic
  sample is locked in the compatibility corpus and read back through the reader on every run.
- G7 (deterministic). The payload depends only on the multiset of rows of `T` and `P`, `L`, `R`
  and `tau`: equal for shuffled rows, for pandas and Arrow inputs (and chunked Arrow), and across
  processes. No timestamp, path, host or version string is in it.
- G8 (value-free, dark). No cell value, label or class key in the document, its `repr`, or any
  error; errors have no exception chain. Every string in the document is a fixed token, the
  schema tag, or a column name that passes the engine's name check (`approve_name`). V1
  behaviour is unchanged.

**Definition of done.** G1 to G8 hold, proved by the acceptance tests of section 8: hand-computed
fixtures for each measure, an independent test-side reference, determinism across row order and
pandas / Arrow, the value-free walk, the reader cells, and the corpus entry; the schema tag is
listed in `docs/compatibility-contract.md`; limits in `docs/what-we-cannot-prove.md`; CHANGELOG
`[Unreleased]` entry.

**Non-goals (recorded follow-ups, not blockers).**

- Attaching the report to run evidence, config keys for the threshold and population, reason
  codes, and showing it in the CLI (slice 2, section 13).
- Population uniqueness estimated from the sample (Pitman, Zayatz, SNB, Dankar's decision rule;
  ARX `RiskModelPopulationUniqueness`) and any journalist or marketer figure derived from such an
  estimate (section 2, "Decision on journalist risk").
- An expert determination. The report states measures and assumptions; it does not judge
  whether a risk is "very small" (45 CFR 164.514(b)(1)), name an anticipated recipient, or
  set a time limit (section 2, OCR guidance).
- Risk of the suppressed rows (they are not released in `D.table`), of
  `generalized_including_suppressed`, or of a release that did not come from the search.
- Attribute-disclosure risk beyond the models met (l and t are reported as met or not, with
  their thresholds; no per-class sensitive figures, which V2-3.2's reports already give).
- ARX's lowest-risk figure (`1 / max f_j`) and its HIPAA identifier scan.
- Changing `SearchResult`, `Released`, `search_lattice`, `apply_search` or the V2-3.2 reports.

**Risk: R2.** Privacy logic whose figures a person may rely on to release data, plus a new
persisted schema (compatibility surface) and new public import paths. Not R3: nothing
deployed, nothing irreversible, no external cost. Rollback: revert the build commits; nothing
calls the package, and no artifact of this schema exists outside the corpus.

## 2. Established solutions

Searches run (2026-11-12, WebSearch and WebFetch): "ARX anonymization risk models prosecutor
journalist marketer RiskModelSampleSummary records at risk threshold"; "El Emam Dankar 2008
protecting privacy using k-anonymity journalist risk marketer risk definitions"; "HHS OCR
guidance expert determination 164.514(b)(1) documentation replicability data source availability
distinguishability anticipated recipient very small risk"; "Dankar El Emam 2012 estimating the
re-identification risk of clinical data sets population uniqueness Pitman Zayatz SNB model
selection ARX". Fetched from the ARX source (`github.com/arx-deidentifier/arx`, master,
Apache-2.0): `risk/RiskModelSampleSummary.java` and `risk/RiskModelPopulationUniqueness.java`.
The HHS page (`www.hhs.gov`) and PubMed Central were outside the session's network allowlist; the
OCR guidance content below is taken from secondary summaries returned by the search (Epstein
Becker Green, Network for Public Health Law, the California CHHS data guide) and from the
guidance's well-known structure, and the build re-checks the cited wording if the host is
allowed. Codebase: the V2-3.x packages and plans, the run-result document, the corpus.

Findings:

- **ARX's sample risk summary** (`RiskModelSampleSummary`, javadoc: "risk measures as proposed by
  El Emam in 'Guide to the De-Identification of Personal Health Information'"). Three attacker
  models, each with records at risk (rA), highest risk (rB) and average risk / success rate (rC):
  - *Prosecutor* (the attacker knows the target is in the release): sample class sizes only.
    `rA = sum{f : 1/f > threshold} / n`, `rB = 1 / min f`, `rC = classes / n`.
  - *Journalist* (the attacker does not know the target is in the release; the release is a
    sample of a known population): sample classes are looked up in the population.
    `rA = sum{f_j : 1/F_j > threshold} / n`, `rB = 1 / min F_j` over the sample's classes,
    `rC = max(J / sum F_j, (1/n) sum f_j / F_j)`.
  - *Marketer* (the attacker wants to re-identify as many records as possible):
    `rC = (1/n) sum f_j / F_j`.
  - Sample classes skip outliers (suppressed records); population classes include every row of
    the superset. When the data has no superset ARX sets `population = sample`, so its journalist
    figures equal the prosecutor figures and marketer equals `J / n`.
  - The threshold comparison is strict (`1d / groupSize > threshold`); ARX notes its effective
    threshold may be exceeded by up to 1% through rounding of its parameter translation.
- **El Emam's definitions** (El Emam and Dankar, "Protecting privacy using k-anonymity", JAMIA
  15(5) 2008, doi 10.1197/jamia.M2716; Dankar and El Emam, "A method for evaluating marketer
  re-identification risk", EDBT/ICDT workshops 2010; El Emam, *Guide to the De-Identification of
  Personal Health Information*, CRC Press 2013). The journalist scenario requires an
  identification database (the population) that the released sample was drawn from; the
  journalist risk depends on population class sizes, not sample ones, and is at most the
  prosecutor risk. The ordering prosecutor >= journalist >= marketer holds for a true
  superset (also stated in Protegrity's risk-metric documentation, which implements the same
  formulas).
- **Population uniqueness estimators** (Dankar, El Emam, Neisa and Roffey, "Estimating the
  re-identification risk of clinical data sets", BMC Med Inform Decis Mak 12:66, 2012). Pitman,
  Zayatz and slide negative binomial (SNB) estimate the number of population uniques from the
  sample's class-size histogram and a sampling fraction; no single estimator is good across
  conditions, so the paper gives a decision rule (Pitman at sampling fraction <= 0.1, else the
  smaller of Zayatz and SNB). ARX implements this in `RiskModelPopulationUniqueness`
  (`PopulationUniquenessModel` PITMAN, ZAYATZ, SNB, DANKAR), driven by an `ARXPopulationModel`
  (a region or a population size) and a numeric solver configuration. It estimates the fraction
  of population uniques, a different quantity from the journalist figures above.
- **OCR guidance on Expert Determination** (HHS Office for Civil Rights, "Guidance Regarding
  Methods for De-identification of Protected Health Information in Accordance with the HIPAA
  Privacy Rule", November 2012, section 2):
  - No numeric level of risk is "very small" universally; the expert sets it for the
    anticipated recipient and the data.
  - The expert documents the methods and results that justify the determination, and tests that
    the risk is no more than very small for the anticipated recipients.
  - Principles for identifying which features are risky: replicability, data source
    availability, distinguishability; the expert then assesses risk.
  - A determination can be time-limited, after which the expert re-examines.
  - What a report must therefore state: which fields were treated as identifying
    (quasi-identifiers), the method applied (generalization, suppression), the risk measures,
    the threshold used and who set it, the attacker assumptions (with or without population
    data), and what the analysis does not cover. The judgement itself is the expert's.

**Decision on journalist risk.** Slice 1 computes journalist and marketer risk only from an
explicitly supplied population table, with ARX's formulas (G2), and refuses to write either
without one (G3). It does not follow ARX's "no superset means population = sample" default:
that default writes the prosecutor figures under the journalist name, which the slice brief
forbids and which overstates what a release-only analysis knows. It does not use the population
uniqueness estimators: they estimate a different quantity (population uniques), need a sampling
fraction or population size the caller would assert, and need a numeric solver (no new
dependencies); Dankar et al. show their error varies widely by condition. A population table is
a real superset the caller holds: the pre-subset table when the release is a subset (Decoy's
`subset` stage), or a registry extract. The build checks it is consistent with being a superset
(every released class present with `F_j >= f_j`) and refuses otherwise. Estimators are a recorded
follow-up.

**Decision on the threshold.** `risk_threshold` is a required argument with no default. OCR says
no number is universally "very small"; a default would choose for the expert. El Emam's commonly
cited thresholds (0.05, 0.075, 0.09, 0.2, the last for a smallest class of 5) are named in the
reference docs as examples only.

**Design follows.** ARX `RiskModelSampleSummary` for every formula (with El Emam's definitions as
the source it cites); ARX's treatment of outliers (sample classes exclude suppressed rows,
population classes include every population row); OCR's documentation expectations for what the
document states (fixed tokens for method, assumptions and exclusions); the engine's
`decoy-run-result/v1` pattern for the versioned document and the compatibility corpus for
locking it.

**Where it departs, and why.**

- No `population = sample` default (decision above).
- The threshold is read as the decimal it prints as (`Fraction(repr(tau))`), not ARX's
  parameter translation with up to 1% rounding slack, so the boundary is exact and the hand
  fixtures pin it (V2-3.3 reads its suppression limit the same way).
- Averages that are sums of ratios (`sum f_j / F_j`) use `math.fsum` (exactly rounded, so the
  order of classes does not change the result); ARX sums doubles in iteration order.
- `records_at_risk` is also given as an exact row count (`rows_at_risk`) beside ARX's fraction.

Custom code is needed because no allowed dependency implements these figures (ARX is Java; no
new dependencies). It is the three attacker summaries over class sizes, the joint sample /
population class lookup, and the document model; class identity, the models and the release are
reused.

## 3. Behaviour

**API (new import paths).**

```python
from decoy_engine.anonymize.search import apply_search, search_lattice
from decoy_engine.anonymize.report import (
    RISK_UTILITY_REPORT_SCHEMA, RiskUtilityReport, RiskUtilityReportRead, build_risk_utility_report,
)
from decoy_engine.quality.anonymity import ReidentificationRiskReport, measure_reidentification_risk

result = search_lattice(df, lattice, models=(KAnonymity(k=5),), suppression_limit=0.02)
released = apply_search(df, lattice, result)
report = build_risk_utility_report(released, lattice, result, risk_threshold=0.2)
report.to_payload()        # JSON-compatible dict, "schema": "risk-utility-report/v1"
RiskUtilityReportRead.model_validate(payload)   # the reader

# with a population (for example the table the release was subset from):
report = build_risk_utility_report(released, lattice, result, risk_threshold=0.2, population=full_df)

# the measure alone, on any table already at the generalization being released:
measure_reidentification_risk(table, ("age", "zip"), risk_threshold=0.2, population=None)
```

**The measure** (`quality.anonymity`, frozen dataclass, `check_report_values` as every V2-3.2
report): `ReidentificationRiskReport(row_count, class_count, risk_threshold, rows_at_risk_prosecutor,
records_at_risk_prosecutor, highest_prosecutor_risk, average_prosecutor_risk, population_rows,
rows_at_risk_journalist, records_at_risk_journalist, highest_journalist_risk,
average_journalist_risk, average_marketer_risk)`; prosecutor fields `None` for an empty table;
population fields `None` without a population. Builtin `int` / `float` / `None` only.

**The document** (`risk-utility-report/v1`; strict writer `RiskUtilityReport`, derived reader
`RiskUtilityReportRead`). Wire shape (field order as written; every number strict):

```json
{
  "schema": "risk-utility-report/v1",
  "method": "full_domain_generalization_with_record_suppression",
  "quasi_identifiers": ["age", "zip"],
  "heights": [3, 5],
  "node": [2, 3],
  "models": [
    {"kind": "k_anonymity", "sensitive": null, "k": 5, "l": null, "c": null, "t": null, "met": true}
  ],
  "search": {"lattice_size": 24, "nodes_evaluated": 17, "metric": "loss"},
  "utility": {"discernibility": 1450231, "loss_metric": 0.41, "non_uniform_entropy": 812.5},
  "suppression": {"rows_input": 10000, "suppression_limit": 0.02, "allowed_suppressed": 200,
                  "rows_suppressed": 143, "rows_released": 9857},
  "classes": {"class_count": 311, "k_min": 5},
  "risk": {
    "risk_threshold": 0.2,
    "population": "not_supplied",
    "population_rows": null,
    "prosecutor": {"highest": 0.2, "average": 0.0316, "records_at_risk": 0.0, "rows_at_risk": 0},
    "journalist": null,
    "marketer": null
  },
  "assumptions": ["prosecutor_target_known_in_release", "suppressed_rows_not_released"],
  "not_established": ["expert_determination", "linkage_with_external_data",
                      "population_risk_without_population", "suppressed_rows_risk"]
}
```

- `method`, `models[].kind`, `search.metric`, `risk.population`, `assumptions[]` and
  `not_established[]` are `Literal` tokens on write (read: any approved identifier, so a token
  added in a later minor reads). Model kinds come from a `kind: ClassVar[str]` on each V2-3.3
  descriptor (one place per fact).
- With a population: `population: "supplied"`, `population_rows` the population's row count,
  `journalist: {highest, average, records_at_risk, rows_at_risk}`, `marketer: {average}`;
  `assumptions` gains `population_is_superset_of_release` and `not_established` drops
  `population_risk_without_population`. The token lists are fixed per case (no free text) and
  written in a stated order.
- `rows_released == 0` (an empty input, or a found node whose suppression limit allowed every
  row to be suppressed): prosecutor figures `null`, `class_count` `0`, `k_min` `null`; with a
  population, journalist and marketer figures `null` too, `population` still `"supplied"`.
- Floats: risks in `[0, 1]`, `AllowInfNan(False)`; counts `int >= 0`; `met` strict `bool`.

**Inputs and checks.** `released` must be a `Released`; `lattice` a `Lattice`; `result` a
`SearchResult` with `found` true; `risk_threshold` `int` or `float` (not `bool`), finite, in
`(0, 1]`; `population` `None`, a `pandas.DataFrame` or a `pyarrow.Table`. `result` must match
`lattice` (columns, heights) and `released` (row counts, suppressed mask equal to the recomputed
one). The builder never modifies its inputs.

**Failure modes.**

| Situation | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| wrong argument types (including `bool` threshold, a non-`Released`, a not-found result) | `TypeError` / `ValueError`, fixed text |
| `risk_threshold` NaN, `<= 0`, `> 1` | `ValueError` naming the parameter |
| `result` from another lattice (columns or heights differ) | `ValueError`, fixed text |
| `released` inconsistent with `result` (row counts, suppressed count, or suppressed mask differs from the recomputation; a released class fails a model) | `ValueError`, fixed text |
| population missing a quasi-identifier column, or repeating one | V2-3.2's `check_columns` `ValueError` naming the column |
| population value a hierarchy does not cover | V2-3.1's `ValueError` from `Lattice.apply` (column name and a row count) |
| a released class absent from the population, or `F_j < f_j` for some class | `ValueError` stating the number of such classes, no value |
| a quasi-identifier or sensitive column name that `approve_name` refuses (path or URL shape, control characters, over 128 characters) | `ValueError` "a column name cannot be written to the report", without the name |
| unhashable or unreadable cells (population) | V2-3.2's value-free `TypeError` naming the column |
| reader: other major, missing `schema`, free text in a token field, a `bool` where a count belongs, a numeric string, a NaN or out-of-range risk | pydantic `ValidationError` with `hide_input_in_errors` (no input echoed) |
| any error | no cell value; `__cause__` and `__context__` are `None` (raised after `except` blocks close) |

## 4. Design notes

**Principles that apply.**

- *Single responsibility.* `quality.anonymity._risk` owns the risk measure (measurement, pure
  function of class sizes); `anonymize.report` owns assembling and versioning the document.
  The report module cannot live in `quality.anonymity`: it reads `SearchResult` and `Released`
  from `anonymize.search`, which imports `quality.anonymity` (the V2-3.2 import walk allows only
  the `decoy_engine.anonymize` root there).
- *One place per fact.* The prosecutor formulas move to `_risk.py` and `measure_k_anonymity`
  calls them; model kind tokens live on the descriptors; loss and suppression figures are copied
  from `SearchResult`; the reader is derived from the writer.
- *Open/closed.* A new privacy model adds its `kind` ClassVar and one `Literal` member; a guard
  test fails when the descriptor tokens and the `Literal` disagree. A new attacker model (for
  example a population-uniqueness estimator later) is a new optional section, additive under
  `/v1`.
- *Deep module.* `build_risk_utility_report(released, lattice, result, *, risk_threshold,
  population=None)` hides the recomputation, the joint population grouping and the document
  shape behind one call.

**Pattern followed.** ARX `RiskModelSampleSummary` (El Emam's risk measures), OCR Expert
Determination documentation; `decoy-run-result/v1` document pattern. Sources in section 2.

**`quality/anonymity/_risk.py`** (new):

1. `prosecutor_highest(min_size)`, `prosecutor_average(classes, rows)`: the two V2-3.2 formulas,
   now called by `measure_k_anonymity` (behaviour identical; its tests stay green unchanged).
2. `measure_reidentification_risk(table, quasi_identifiers, *, risk_threshold, population=None)`:
   - checks names (`check_columns` on the table and, if given, the population), the threshold
     (`check_unit_interval` plus `> 0`);
   - classes of the table: `equivalence_classes(read_columns(table, qi), qi)`;
   - with a population: both frames read with `read_columns`, each column cast to `object`
     (so a dtype change on concatenation cannot merge or split values: `1` and `1.0` stay two
     values, as V2-3.2's type families say), concatenated, grouped once with
     `equivalence_classes`; `f_j` and `F_j` are `bincount`s of the sample and population parts of
     the joint ids; a sample class with `F_j < f_j` (including `F_j = 0`) refuses;
   - at-risk test: `f * Fraction(repr(tau)) < 1` per class size (exact; equivalent to
     `1/f > tau`), evaluated once per distinct size;
   - averages: prosecutor `J / n` (one division); journalist `max(J / sum F_j, fsum(f_j / F_j) / n)`;
     marketer `fsum(f_j / F_j) / n`.

**`anonymize/report/`** (new sub-package):

- `_model.py`: `RISK_UTILITY_REPORT_SCHEMA = "risk-utility-report/v1"`; named value-free
  types (`SchemaTag` accepting only this tag, `ColumnName` via `approve_name`); strict `Count`,
  `Risk` (`float`, strict, `0 <= x <= 1`, finite), `Flag`; `Literal` token sets; the nested
  models of the wire shape above, `extra="forbid"`, frozen, `hide_input_in_errors=True`;
  `RiskUtilityReportRead = derive_read_model(RiskUtilityReport)`.
- `_build.py`: `build_risk_utility_report`:
  1. argument checks (types, threshold, `result.found`, lattice match, row counts), before any
     data is read;
  2. `fs = FrequencySet.of(released.generalized_including_suppressed, lattice.columns,
     result.models)`; `failing = fs.failing(result.models)`; the recomputed mask
     `fs.row_mask(failing)` must equal `released.suppressed` exactly; per model `met = not
     (model.failing_classes(fs) & ~failing).any()` (by construction true; false refuses);
  3. population (if any): `lattice.apply(population, result.node)`;
  4. `risk = measure_reidentification_risk(released.table, lattice.columns,
     risk_threshold=..., population=generalized_population)`;
  5. assemble `RiskUtilityReport` from `result`, `risk` and the token lists; column names checked
     with `approve_name` first (a refused name raises the fixed-text `ValueError`).

**Reusing the run-result derivation (open question Q1).** `run_result/_model.py` holds
`ValueFree` and `_derive_read`, the derivation that turns a strict writer into a tolerant reader
(enums to tokens, extra fields ignored, `schema` tag required). It is generic except for one
`reason_payload` substitution this document never uses. Copying it would fork the
compatibility rule; importing `_model` from another package is recurring defect 6. The plan
exports two names from `decoy_engine.run_result`: `ValueFree` and `derive_read_model` (the same
function object as `_derive_read`; no code moves, no behaviour change; not added to
`decoy_engine.__all__`). The alternative is to move both into a neutral public module both
packages import; that touches more V1 lines for the same result.

**Determinism.** Every figure is a function of class sizes (row-order invariant) and of fields
copied from `SearchResult` (G3 of V2-3.3). Exact integers and `Fraction` comparisons, single
divisions, and `math.fsum` for sums of ratios (exactly rounded, order-free). Lists in the
document have a fixed order: quasi-identifiers and heights in `lattice.columns` order, models
in `result.models` order, token lists in a constant order. The pandas / Arrow route only changes
how columns are read, through V2-3.2's lossless `read_columns`.

**Memory and time.** `n` input rows, `N` population rows, `q` quasi-identifiers. The
recomputation is one `FrequencySet.of` over the generalized table (V2-3.3's `apply_search`
cost: read columns, `8n` bytes of class ids), the measure one grouping of the released rows; with
a population, one `Lattice.apply` of the population (`O(N)` per column and level of the node) and
one joint grouping of `n + N` rows as `object` columns (the object cast is the extra cost: about
`8 (n + N) q` bytes of pointers plus boxed values). Linear in rows; no per-class Python loop
except the per-distinct-size threshold test. The build records one measured run at 200,000
sample and 1,000,000 population rows (warm, three runs, min and max); nothing timing-based is
asserted in T1.

**House style.** Typed descriptors and pydantic models; no flag arguments (the population is
data, not a switch: its absence is a stated case in the document); comments say why.
Methodology `Pattern:` lines in `_risk.py`, `anonymize/report/__init__.py`, `_build.py`,
registered in `docs/methodology-registry.yaml`.

**Public surface and compatibility.** New package `decoy_engine.anonymize.report` (pre-GA,
unstable Python API) and new names in `decoy_engine.quality.anonymity.__all__`
(`ReidentificationRiskReport`, `measure_reidentification_risk`) and
`decoy_engine.run_result.__all__` (`ValueFree`, `derive_read_model`). Not in
`decoy_engine.__all__`; not in the strategy catalog; `import decoy_engine` does not load
`anonymize`. The document tag `risk-utility-report/v1` joins the compatibility contract's
section 3.1 list: under `/v1` only optional fields may be added; a shape change mints `/v2` with
a kept `v1` reader. Pre-GA, so the tag can still be hard-replaced before GA (contract section 1),
but the corpus entry makes any change visible.

## 9. Known limits (also in `docs/what-we-cannot-prove.md` where marked *)

- \* The figures describe the released rows against attackers who match on the named
  quasi-identifiers only. A column not named as a quasi-identifier is assumed unknown to the
  attacker; that choice (OCR's replicability, data source availability, distinguishability) is
  the caller's, and a wrong choice makes every figure optimistic.
- \* Journalist and marketer risk are only as good as the population supplied. The build checks
  the population is consistent with being a superset of the release (every class present, at
  least as large); it cannot check that the population is the real one an attacker would use.
  Without a population the report states it and gives no such figure.
- \* No figure is an expert determination. "Very small" risk, the anticipated recipient, and a
  time limit are judgements the report leaves to the expert; the threshold is the caller's.
- \* The suppressed rows' risk is not reported (they are not in `Released.table`). Releasing
  `generalized_including_suppressed` publishes rows these figures never covered.
- Attribute disclosure is covered only as far as the l and t models met; the report gives no
  per-class sensitive-value figures.
- Population uniqueness estimators (Pitman, Zayatz, SNB, Dankar) are not implemented.
- Column names appear in the document; a name refused by `approve_name` makes the build refuse
  rather than write it (slice 2 decides whether to write positional names instead).
- The report recomputes the suppression mask and groups the released table again (one extra
  `O(n)` pass each); with a population, the joint grouping casts to `object`, which costs memory
  for wide numeric quasi-identifiers.
- The threshold is compared exactly as a decimal, which can differ from ARX's displayed
  results at the boundary (ARX's parameter translation allows up to 1% rounding).
