### The defect: a parse-stage parent quarantine publishes orphans under `remap`, `preserve` and `warn`

The engine supports four orphan policies: `OrphanPolicyLiteral = Literal["preserve", "remap", "warn", "fail"]` (`src/decoy_engine/config/_relationships.py:21`; the plan types use the same set). There is no `cascade`.

r5 refuses only a **format-stage** quarantine on a relationship parent (`post_mask_parent_row_quarantine`). A **parse-stage** quarantine drops the parent row before the key maps are built. With `fail`, the dropped row turns its children into orphans and `orphan_fk_violation` fires, which is the case r5 tested. With the other three policies, the same engine-made orphans are let through and published:

| Shape | Policy | full_frame | sequential | auto (runs sequential) | out_of_core |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| single, composite, self-referencing | `fail` | `orphan_fk_violation` | same | same | refused (`row_quarantine`) |
| single, composite, self-referencing | `remap` | **published, 1 orphan** | **published, 1 orphan** | **published, 1 orphan** | refused |
| single, composite, self-referencing | `preserve` | **published, 1 orphan** | **published, 1 orphan** | **published, 1 orphan** | refused |
| single, composite, self-referencing | `warn` | **published, 1 orphan** | **published, 1 orphan** | **published, 1 orphan** | refused |

Sources: `f06-r6/parse_stage_orphans_single.{py,txt}` (single column, all four policies, both stages) and `f06-r6/parse_stage_orphans_composite_selfref.{py,txt}`. The format stage is refused on every route and under every policy (same files), so the r5 refusal is consistent. It just does not cover this case.

Why these three policies publish: on both routes they are built to tolerate orphans that exist in the source. `remap` re-hashes the orphan key, and `preserve` keeps it verbatim (`f06-r6/source_orphan_policy_control.{py,txt}`: a real source orphan is published under both). The difference here is that the source is consistent. The engine creates the orphan by quarantining the parent. Three consequences make this more than a semantics question:

* Under `preserve`, the child publishes the **raw source key** (`"1"`) of a parent row that really exists and was quarantined. That parent's identifier leaves the job unmasked.
* `quality_metrics["declared_casting"]["completeness"]` reports `complete` while children point at a removed row. Only `warn` emits an `orphan_fk` `QualityWarning`; `remap` and `preserve` say nothing (`f06-r6/parse_stage_warnings_and_completeness.{py,txt}`).
* The same orphans appear on every route that can quarantine (full_frame and sequential; auto picks sequential). This is a route-independent gap, not a single-route slip.

Not decided here: is the answer (a) refuse a parse-stage quarantine on a relationship parent unless `orphan_policy: fail`, the same config-only shape as r5; (b) re-run the orphan check against the post-quarantine parent set; or (c) accept `remap`/`warn` and refuse only `preserve`, because of the raw-key leak? That changes the refusal surface, which the brief reserves for the owner.

  * unified slice declines `profile.relationships` (`_unified_slice_admission.py:253`);

  * the multi-table split requires `not config.get("relationships")` (`_pipeline_multi_table.py:141`) and never runs a declared job (`declared_casting_full_frame`).

  For relational shapes, these three cells can only assert "lane not taken, actual route = X".
* Forced mode exists only for `full_frame`, `sequential` and `out_of_core`: `execution_mode: Literal["auto", "sequential", "full_frame", "out_of_core"]` (`_pipeline.py:129`). Chunked, unified slice, mixed and the multi-table split are auto-only lanes, so they have no forced variant.
* `mixed` needs a generated parent. A declared-casting mask parent with mask children is not a shape the mixed route admits (`_mixed_static.admit_mixed_static`).
* The code has no FK-shape enumeration. The shapes come from the relationship schema: endpoint column-tuple length 1 or greater than 1, and parent table equal to child table.
