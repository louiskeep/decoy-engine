## 1. FRAME

**Problem.** V2-3 enforces k-anonymity. Its first tool (V2-3.1) is generalization: a numeric
quasi-identifier such as age or income is replaced by an interval label. That destroys the
column's numeric type and much of its utility (means, regressions, correlations). The standard
alternative in statistical disclosure control for continuous quasi-identifiers is
microaggregation: partition the records into groups of at least k that are close in the
quasi-identifier space and publish each group's mean instead of each record's values. Every
record then shares its quasi-identifier values with at least k-1 others, so the output is
k-anonymous on those columns, while the columns stay numeric and the column totals are kept. The
engine has nothing that does this.

**Guarantee (the whole promise).** For `microaggregate(table, quasi_identifiers, k=k)` that
returns (no refusal), with `n` rows in `table`:

- G1 (group sizes). The rows are partitioned into groups. If `n == 0` there are no groups.
  Otherwise every group has between `k` and `2k - 1` rows; every group except the last one formed
  has exactly `k` rows (the last-group rule, section 3).
- G2 (k-anonymity on the aggregated columns). Every row of a group gets the same output value in
  every quasi-identifier column, bit for bit (one centroid vector written to all members). So
  every equivalence class over the quasi-identifiers in the output has at least `k` rows (two
  groups can share a centroid; that only makes a class larger).
- G3 (centroid). A group's output value in a column is the arithmetic mean of its members' input
  values in that column, computed in float64. Column totals are therefore kept up to float64
  rounding.
- G4 (MDAV). The partition is the one the MDAV heuristic produces (Domingo-Ferrer and Torra 2005,
  as implemented in sdcMicro, section 2) over the quasi-identifiers standardised by their
  population standard deviation, with ties broken by row position (section 4).
- G5 (determinism). Same input table (same values, same row order), same `k`: same output and
  report, bit for bit, in any process on the same platform and library versions. No randomness,
  no key, no seed.
- G6 (no mutation, no side channel). The input table is not modified. Columns not named as
  quasi-identifiers are returned unchanged (same values, dtype, order, index). Errors and the
  report carry no cell value; errors carry no exception chain.

**Findings.**

- Microaggregation (Defays and Nanopoulos 1993; Domingo-Ferrer and Mateo-Sanz, "Practical
  data-oriented microaggregation for statistical disclosure control", IEEE TKDE 14(1), 2002)
  partitions records into groups of at least k and replaces each record by its group's centroid.
  Optimal multivariate microaggregation is NP-hard (Oganian and Domingo-Ferrer 2001), so tools use
  heuristics. Domingo-Ferrer and Mateo-Sanz 2002 show optimal groups have between k and 2k-1
  records and measure information loss as `SSE / SST` on standardised data (SSE: sum of squared
  distances of records to their group centroid; SST: to the overall centroid).
- MDAV (Domingo-Ferrer and Torra, "Ordinal, continuous and heterogeneous k-anonymity through
  microaggregation", Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery 11(2), 2005; also the fixed-size
  heuristic in Hundepool et al., *Statistical Disclosure Control*, Wiley 2012, section 3.4):
  while at least 3k records remain, compute the centroid `c`, the record `x_r` farthest from `c`,
  the record `x_s` farthest from `x_r`; form a group of `x_r` and its k-1 nearest records, then a
  group of `x_s` and its k-1 nearest among the rest. If between 2k and 3k-1 remain, form one
  group around `x_r` and put the rest (k to 2k-1) in a final group. If fewer than 2k remain, they
  form the final group.
- sdcMicro `microaggregation(method = "mdav", aggr = 3)` calls `Mdav.cpp` (OECD, adapted by
  Kowarik). What the code does, which we follow:
  - weights each variable by `1 / variance` (population variance over non-missing values; weight
    1 if the variance is 0), so distances are squared Euclidean on standardised data;
  - the loop runs while `remaining >= 2k`; it forms two groups if `remaining - 2k >= k`, else
    one; the rest (fewer than 2k) is the final group, averaged over all its rows;
  - the first group is the k rows nearest `x_r` (which includes `x_r`); the second is the k rows
    nearest `x_s` among those left, where `x_s` is the row farthest from `x_r` chosen before the
    first group is removed;
  - farthest picks use a strict `>` over rows in row order, so a tie goes to the earliest row;
  - the published values are the arithmetic mean of the raw (unstandardised) values; `measure`
    is ignored for `mdav`;
  - distances are single-precision (`typedef float TDist`); the k nearest are found with a
    65,536-bucket hash sort in which rows at an equal distance come out latest-first;
  - missing values (the R wrapper passes a sentinel; `Mdav.h` initialises `g_MissingValue` to
    `-1.0` before `Mdav.cpp` overwrites it from that argument) get a fixed distance (squared
    range) and are left out of means;
  - variance is one-pass, `E[x^2] - mean^2`, which cancels for offset columns: for
    `[1e9, 1e9 + 1, 1e9 + 2]` it gives 0.0, so sdcMicro gives such a column weight 1.
- sdcMicro's single-variable path uses `mafast` (sort-based), not MDAV. ARX lists
  microaggregation (mean as aggregate function) among its transformations, applied per attribute
  within equivalence classes formed by generalization, not as a clustering heuristic; it is not
  the reference for this slice.
- V-MDAV (Solanas and Martinez-Balleste, COMPSTAT 2006) extends groups up to 2k-1 when a
  neighbour is close (gamma threshold); F-MDAV (Rodriguez-Hoyos, Estrada-Jimenez, Rebollo-Monedero,
  Mezher, Parra-Arnau, Forne, EAAI 2020) speeds MDAV for large data. Both recorded as follow-ups.

**Source followed.** MDAV as defined by Domingo-Ferrer and Torra 2005, with sdcMicro `Mdav.cpp`'s
concrete choices: standardisation by population variance, the two-groups-per-pass loop with the
`>= 2k` / `>= 3k` boundaries above, `x_s` chosen before the first group is removed, raw-value
means. Information loss: `SSE / SST` on standardised data (Domingo-Ferrer and Mateo-Sanz 2002).

**Where we differ from sdcMicro, and why.**

| sdcMicro | Here | Why |
|---|---|---|
| float32 distances | float64 | Fewer accidental ties; no cost in numpy. Results equal sdcMicro's whenever no two candidate distances are within float32 rounding of each other (the fixtures are built that way). |
| equal distances in the k-nearest pick: latest row first | earliest row first (by position) | One stated rule (row position) for every tie; reproducible from the docstring. A test pins it. |
| missing values: fixed distance, left out of means | refused | A null cell left in place would form its own small class (V2-3.2's null policy), breaking G2; imputing is a modelling choice the caller must make. |
| one-pass variance `E[x^2] - mean^2` (0.0 for `[1e9, 1e9+1, 1e9+2]`) | centre, then two-pass `numpy.std`; constancy decided exactly | An offset column (epoch seconds) keeps its scale; no spurious weight 1. |
| one variable: `mafast` | MDAV | One algorithm and one guarantee for every input; for one variable MDAV still satisfies G1 to G3. |
| `n < k`: one group of `n` | refused | Output with fewer than k rows cannot be k-anonymous; returning it would break G1. |

**Why custom code.** No maintained Python package for MDAV is an allowed dependency (no new
dependencies; R and sdcMicro cannot be used from the engine). The algorithm is about 80 lines of
numpy; the rest is input checking and the report.

## 3. Behaviour

**Signature.**

```python
def microaggregate(
    table: pd.DataFrame | pa.Table,
    quasi_identifiers: Sequence[str],
    *,
    k: int,
) -> MicroaggregationResult
```

**Algorithm (on the standardised matrix `Z`, one row per record, rows in input order).**

```text
remaining = all rows
while |remaining| >= 2k:
    c   = mean of Z over remaining
    x_r = remaining row farthest from c           (ties: earliest row)
    d_r = squared distance of each remaining row to x_r
    x_s = remaining row farthest from x_r          (ties: earliest row)
    G   = the k remaining rows nearest x_r by d_r (ties: earliest rows); emit G; remove G
    if |remaining| >= k  and  the pass began with >= 3k rows:
        H = the k remaining rows nearest x_s (ties: earliest rows); emit H; remove H
if remaining: emit remaining as the last group
```

Groups are numbered in emission order. Each group's centroid is the mean of its members' raw
float64 values, members summed in ascending row order.

**Behaviour and failure modes.**

| Input | Result |
|---|---|
| `n == 0` | the table back with each quasi-identifier column cast to float64; report with `group_count = 0`, sizes `None`, `sse = sst = information_loss = 0.0` |
| `1 <= n < k` | `ValueError("table has fewer rows than k")` |
| `k <= n < 2k` | one group of all `n` rows |
| `2k <= n < 3k` | two groups: `k` rows around `x_r`, then the rest (`k` to `2k-1`) |
| `n >= 3k` | passes of two groups of `k`; final group `k` to `2k-1` |
| `n > MAX_ROWS` (50,000) | `ValueError("table has more rows than microaggregation supports")`, before any computation; message gives the limit, not the data |
| `k` not exactly `int` (bool, float, str, numpy int) | `TypeError("k must be an int (not bool, float or str)")` |
| `k < 2` | `ValueError("k must be at least 2")` (k = 1 changes nothing and claims nothing) |
| `quasi_identifiers` a `str`, not a list or tuple, empty, holding a non-`str`, or with a duplicate | `TypeError` / `ValueError` |
| a named column missing, or present twice in the table | `ValueError` naming the column (column names are schema, not data) |
| a quasi-identifier column not numeric: bool, object (even if it holds numbers), string, categorical, datetime, timedelta, decimal, complex | `TypeError("column 'x' is not a numeric column")`; numeric means pandas int/uint/float, nullable `Int*`/`UInt*`/`Float*`, and `ArrowDtype` or Arrow int/uint/float (including float16) |
| a null in a quasi-identifier (`NaN`, `None`, `pd.NA`, Arrow null) | `ValueError("N rows have a null quasi-identifier; microaggregation needs complete values")` with the row count only |
| `inf` or `-inf` | `ValueError("N rows have a non-finite quasi-identifier")` |
| a non-constant column whose mean or population standard deviation is not finite (overflow to `inf`, or `nan`), or whose z-scores are not finite | `ValueError("column 'x' is too large to standardise")` |
| a group mean that is not finite (unreachable once standardisation succeeded; a guard so the report never refuses accepted input) | `ValueError("quasi-identifier values are too large to aggregate")` |
| a constant column (decided exactly: every value equal to the first) | dropped from the distance matrix; written back as its exact input value in float64 (a pass-through, not a computed mean: the mean of five 0.1s is not 0.1 in float); information loss exactly 0.0 |
| all rows identical | groups by the loop and tie rule; every centroid equals the common value; `sst = sse = 0`, `information_loss = 0.0` |
| integer column with values above 2^53 in magnitude | converted to float64 (the nearest float); stated in the docstring and known limits |

**Output.** `MicroaggregationResult(table, report)`, frozen.

- `table`: same type as the input (`pandas.DataFrame` or `pyarrow.Table`), a new object. Quasi-
  identifier columns are float64 (pandas: numpy `float64` Series with the input's index and name;
  Arrow: `pa.float64()` field with the same name and position, field metadata not kept); every
  other column is the input's own column object (pandas: from `copy(deep=False)`, then the
  quasi-identifier columns assigned on the copy; Arrow: `set_column`). The input is never written.
- Integer columns come out as float64, the exact centroid. This is sdcMicro's behaviour (its
  output is R `double`). A caller who needs integers can round the column afterwards: rounding a
  value that is constant within each group keeps every group intact, so G1 and G2 still hold;
  only the column total (G3) moves.
- `report: MicroaggregationReport` (frozen, `slots=True`, value-free):

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `row_count: int` | `n` |
| `k: int` | the `k` used |
| `quasi_identifiers: tuple[str, ...]` | the columns, in the order given |
| `group_count: int` | number of groups |
| `group_size_min: int \| None`, `group_size_max: int \| None` | `None` when `n == 0` |
| `group_size_distribution: tuple[tuple[int, int], ...]` | (size, number of groups), ascending, as V2-3.2's class-size distribution |
| `sse: float` | within-group sum of squares on the standardised data |
| `sst: float` | total sum of squares on the standardised data (equals `n` times the number of non-constant columns) |
| `information_loss: float` | `sse / sst`, in `[0, 1]`; `0.0` when `sst == 0` (Domingo-Ferrer and Mateo-Sanz 2002; sdcMicro's documented IL measures are `dUtility`'s IL1s and eigenvalue-based ones, not this) |
| `column_information_loss: tuple[float, ...]` | per column, `SSE_j / SST_j`: the pooled within-group variance (divisor n) on the standardised scale, where SST_j = n (the "within-group variance" the sprint asks for); `0.0` for a constant column |

`__post_init__` refuses (with `ValueError`, value-free) a non-finite float, a negative count, or
an `information_loss` outside `[0, 1]`. `sse <= sst` holds mathematically (the mean minimises the
within-group sum); `_report.py` computes `information_loss` as `min(sse / sst, 1.0)` only to absorb
float rounding of order 1e-15, and a test asserts `sse <= sst * (1 + 1e-12)` so a real excess is
caught before the clamp.

## 4. Design notes

**Principles that apply.**

- *Single responsibility.* Three concerns, three modules: input checking and table I/O
  (`_inputs.py`), the partition on a float64 matrix (`_mdav.py`, no pandas or Arrow), the report
  (`_report.py`). `_api.py` composes them. The algorithm module sees only a numpy matrix, so it is
  tested on matrices directly and any later variant (V-MDAV) is one sibling module with the same
  signature.
- *Deep module.* Public surface: one function, two frozen result types, one constant.
- Open/closed for variants: not applied now (one variant; an abstraction with one implementation
  is premature). The seam is the function signature `mdav_groups(z, k) -> NDArray[int64]`.

**Pattern followed.** MDAV (Domingo-Ferrer and Torra 2005) with sdcMicro `Mdav.cpp`'s choices;
SSE/SST information loss (Domingo-Ferrer and Mateo-Sanz 2002). Cited in the `Pattern:` lines of
`_mdav.py` and `_report.py` and in the registry.

**API example.**

```python
from decoy_engine.anonymize.microaggregation import microaggregate

result = microaggregate(df, ["age", "income"], k=5)
out = result.table            # new DataFrame; age and income are float64 group means
result.report.group_size_min  # 5
result.report.information_loss  # e.g. 0.07
```

**Standardisation (decided, cited).** Decide constancy exactly (`(col == col[0]).all()`) and
drop constant columns from the distance matrix. For the others, `z = (x - mean) / std` with the
population standard deviation (`numpy.std`, `ddof=0`, two-pass) over all rows. Scaling is
sdcMicro's `1 / variance` weight on squared differences and the standardised scale on which
Domingo-Ferrer and Mateo-Sanz define IL; without it a column in dollars outweighs a column in
years. Centring does not change exact distances but keeps the digits: uncentred z-scores of an
epoch-like column (offset 1e15) are about 1e14 and lose the differences between rows. Refuse a
non-finite mean, standard deviation or z-score. Consequences tested: multiplying a column by a
positive constant, or adding 1e9 or 1e15 to it, does not change the partition; a constant 0.1
column passes through exactly (section 8).

**Ties (the only order dependence).** `np.argmax` returns the first maximum, which is the earliest
remaining row because the remaining rows are kept in ascending row order. The k nearest are
chosen in O(m): `t = np.partition(d, k - 1)[k - 1]`; take every row with `d < t`, then the
earliest rows with `d == t` until there are k. With no exact ties among the compared distances,
the partition does not depend on row order (tested on continuous random data); with ties, the
stated rule decides.

**Determinism.** No randomness, no hashing, no dict ordering. Floating sums are over arrays in a
fixed order. Same input and platform: same bits (tested in two processes).

**Whole-frame operation; memory and time.** Microaggregation depends on all rows at once: the
groups for a chunk are not the groups for the whole table. The function takes a resident table
and is not chunk-independent; a later wiring slice must call it on the whole table (or by strata,
by design). Memory: the standardised matrix and one working copy, `2 * 8 * n * p` bytes, plus
O(n) index and distance arrays; the input columns are read once with `to_numpy(dtype=float64)`.
Time: each pass costs O(m * p) for m remaining rows and removes 2k rows, so O(n^2 * p / k) in all.
A single-group pass (2k to 3k-1 rows left) skips the `x_s` search. Measured on this machine
with the built code (normal data): k = 2, p = 4: 10,000 rows 2.1 s, 50,000 rows 49 s; k = 3,
p = 3: 50,000 rows 31 s. `MAX_ROWS = 50_000` bounds a call at under a minute at the most
expensive allowed k (2); the refusal comes before any computation; the timings are in the package
docstring. Distances stay in the plain `(diff * diff).sum(axis=1)` form: incremental or BLAS
forms (`|a|^2 - 2ab + |b|^2`) change float bits and so ties. Larger tables need F-MDAV or strata
(follow-up).

**Reading columns.** pandas: each column is checked with `pd.api.types` (`is_bool_dtype` first,
then `is_numeric_dtype`, rejecting `complex`); nullable and Arrow-backed columns are read with
`to_numpy(dtype="float64", na_value=np.nan)` after the null count is taken from `isna()`. Arrow:
`pa.types.is_integer` / `is_floating`, cast to float64 (`safe=False`), nulls from `is_null`, and
NaN counted with nulls (one refusal, one count). Both routes
produce the same float64 matrix, so they agree by construction; a test proves it.

**Errors.** `TypeError` and `ValueError` (as V2-3.1 and V2-3.2); fixed text plus counts, limits
and column names; raised outside any `except` block (recurring defect 3). No new exception class
(so `test_exception_hierarchy.py` is untouched). No reason codes.

**Where each fact lives.** The partition rule and tie rule: `_mdav.py`. Standardisation:
`_mdav.standardise`, used by both the partition and the report (one function, so the report's
scale is the partition's scale). `MAX_ROWS` and the input rules: `_inputs.py`. The report's
fields and invariants: `_report.py`. The public contract: the package docstring.

## 9. Known limits

- Disclosure microaggregation does not prevent (for `docs/what-we-cannot-prove.md`): it gives
  k-anonymity on the named columns only. Centroids are published, so a group whose members are
  close (or identical) reveals their values almost exactly; an attacker who knows k-1 members'
  values computes the last one (`k * centroid - sum`); the sensitive attributes of a group may be
  homogeneous (l-diversity and t-closeness are V2-3.2's measures, not enforced here); columns not
  named stay as they are.
- MDAV is a heuristic: groups are not optimal, and the last group can join distant records (the
  hand fixture's last group mixes (2,3) and (10,12)).
- Whole-frame and at most `MAX_ROWS` rows; O(n^2 p / k) time.
- Integer columns become float64; integers above 2^53 in magnitude lose precision in the
  conversion.
- Results equal sdcMicro's except where sdcMicro's float32 distances or its latest-first tie
  order would choose differently, and except for missing values, n < k and the one-variable path
  (section 2).
- Bitwise reproducibility is per platform and library version, not across them.
